- **2026-10-20 — Context:** giant piloted machine in a future first-person workshop game; plausible fictional mechanics; internal skeleton with armor; current focus explicitly limited to robot design.
- **2026-10-20 — Silhouette:** 18 m height; crisp angular plates; rear-leg mechanical exposure focused on thrusters; elongated toes.
- **2026-10-20 — Thruster concept:** aesthetic role; calf placement; vertical pair; mostly enclosed engine bodies.
- **2026-10-20 — Foot:** chisel toe; hinged forefoot; short substantial heel; visible ankle joint with side guards.
- **2026-10-20 — Foot construction and finish:** rear chassis and hinged forefoot frame; two-axis ankle with visible housings; segmented composite grip pads; visible ankle actuators; clean armor and recessed fasteners.
- **2026-10-20 — Calf:** integrated athletic shape; continuous shin beam with separate rear thruster cradle; circular nozzle bells; separate openings with removable bridge panel; slightly rearward/downward nozzle orientation.
- **2026-10-20 — Documentation:** created this record at the user's request to preserve decisions as the design develops.
- **2026-10-20 — Knee:** single horizontal hinge axle in a forked thigh frame; separate bearings, retaining caps, and brackets; compact tapered kneecap on an upper-shin bracket; visible circular bearing caps inside angular guards; paired partially exposed rear-side actuators with upper mounts under thigh armor; central rear knee left open above the calf thruster enclosure.
- **2026-10-20 — Thigh:** athletic upper thigh slightly broader than the calf, tapering toward the knee; central box-section spar with detachable knee fork and separate armor/actuator brackets; broad front plate, separate inner/outer side plates, and two narrow rear covers; recessed rear service channel showing frame and protected cables above the exposed knee actuators.
- **2026-10-20 — Hip:** user selected a ball-and-socket joint allowing forward swing and splay, with the ball on the leg and the socket in the torso's pelvis. The earlier nested-pivot/rotary-bearing proposal was not adopted. Short front guards, small hinged outer hip plates, and a narrow visible gap above the thigh armor were accepted. Ball attachment, socket retention, twist, and actuation remain unresolved.
- **2026-10-20 — Hip construction:** user preferred the ball to be part of the thigh skeleton; recorded as an integral ball-and-neck upper end, with fabrication details open. The separate bolted-on ball proposal was not adopted. User accepted the bolted two-piece socket retaining ring and limited thigh twist through the ball joint with mechanical stops. Hip actuation remains unresolved.
- **2026-10-20 — Pelvis and waist:** braced pelvis bridge with separately bolted left/right socket housings and central waist mount; compact angled hip actuators with jointed ends, mostly sheltered by the guards, attached to thigh lugs using removable pins; rotary waist bearing with a forward/backward pivot above it. Actuator count, drive technology, exact geometry, and movement limits remain open.
- **2026-10-20 — Chest, abdomen, and cockpit:** two broad angular pectoral plates divided by a central sternum section; torso tapers toward the waist; three overlapping front abdominal plates with narrow side gaps; single-pilot cockpit centered in the chest above the waist articulation; upward-hinged central sternum hatch opens independently of the pectoral plates.
- **2026-10-20 — Torso frame and cockpit construction:** bolted rear spine, two angular side frames, and shoulder crossmember surround the cockpit; separate enclosed cockpit capsule on shock-isolating mounts, installed before front armor; slightly reclined pilot seat with harness, two hand controls, and foot pedals; external cameras feed one main front display and two angled side displays; chest hatch remains opaque and armored.
- **2026-10-20 — Rear torso:** user chose a simple compact thruster backpack; cylindrical power cartridge plugging directly into the torso skeleton; rear access, with the backpack itself proposed to hinge “vertically” and reveal the core; externally visible vertical vents parallel to the spine down to the pelvis; and the recommended pair of lower-backpack circular thruster bells. Exact hinge direction, core orientation/removal path, and vent arrangement across the waist remain unresolved. The earlier backpack-contained power cradle and discrete upper-rear vent-module layout were not adopted.
- **2026-10-20 — Rear access and vents:** backpack opening confirmed as upward rotation about a top-edge hinge; power cartridge oriented front-to-back for straight rearward removal from a keyed torso socket; two tall recessed vent strips remain visible beside the closed backpack and continue to the pelvis in aligned upper/lower sections separated at the moving waist.
- **2026-10-20 — Backpack hardware:** paired top hinge brackets with removable pins and one support strut on each side; two recessed lower-corner latches and torso locating blocks; keyed cartridge guide rails and a rear retaining flange secured with captive bolts; protected flexible cable/service loops near the upper hinge, clear of the core removal path.
- **2026-10-20 — Shoulder:** ball integral to the upper-arm skeleton, socket on the torso shoulder crossmember, and two-piece retaining ring; user selected a boxy pauldron with rigid mounting to the arm and a narrow mechanical gap. The compact independently hinged cap proposal was not adopted. Right shoulder mirrors left; armor clearances and movement ranges remain to be resolved.
- **2026-10-20 — Upper arm and elbow:** slim angular upper arm tapering mildly toward the elbow; central box-section beam with integral shoulder ball, detachable elbow fork, and front/rear armor shells; single transverse elbow axle with separate bearings and circular retaining caps within angular guards; paired compact actuators in rear-side upper-arm channels, mostly covered, with short rod sections visible beside the elbow.
- **2026-10-20 — Forearm and wrist:** angular gauntlet slightly fuller than the upper arm and tapering to the wrist; central beam with removable service tray, outer armor shell, and inner service cover; wrist ball on the hand stem, socket in the forearm cuff, and bolted two-piece retaining collar; forward/backward flexion, sideways tilt, and limited twist; short angular cuff with a narrow gap exposing part of the wrist stem.
- **2026-10-20 — Hand layout:** four fingers and an opposable thumb in humanlike proportions; chunky angular, beveled, flat-ended digits with three segments per finger and two main thumb segments on a pivoting base; rigid palm chassis incorporating wrist stem and ball, separate bolted finger-base blocks, and removable back armor; composite palm/finger grip pads and small armor plates over finger backs and knuckles.
- **2026-10-20 — Hand mechanisms:** transverse finger-hinge pins with bushings and recessed retaining caps; separate geared rotary-drive cartridge at each bending joint for independent movement; limited spread at finger bases and two-axis thumb opposition; protected palm wiring with a local connector for each digit, allowing digit removal through connector and base-bolt access behind the back-of-hand plate.
- **2026-10-20 — Head direction:** user specified a primarily aesthetic, intimidating samurai-style helmet containing only a few sensors. Detailed mask, crest, sensor count/layout, internal frame, and neck remain open; the earlier helmet/eye/V-antenna suggestions were not accepted as a package.
- **2026-10-20 — Head appearance and construction:** user accepted angular samurai helmet with pronounced brow and flared stepped guards, stern cheek/nose/chin mask, paired upward-swept ornamental crest blades, two recessed eye cameras, and one forehead range sensor. User specified assembly from torso neck spoke to head baseplate, sides, top, sensors, front faceplate, mask, and crown, in that order. Structural top shell and final decorative crown are recorded separately; neck motion, panel supports, rear closure, and sensor carrier remain open.
- **2026-10-20 — Head mounting:** fixed keyed neck spoke with central wiring passage and bolted baseplate; two side-shell assemblies wrap around to form the rear and carry the flared guards; three sensor modules on a common carrier installed through the open front; locating tabs and recessed bolts attach panels to short baseplate uprights; faceplate, mask, and crown have distinct attachment points.
- **2026-10-20 — Documentation structure:** split the design record into a short master index, shared rules, component specifications with their open questions, reference-image requirements, and this history. - **2026-10-20 — Rear foot chassis breakdown:** user accepted the recommended separate-piece frame: two longitudinal side rails, one front crossmember, one rear crossmember, and a separate bolted ankle-bearing cradle, totaling five main structural pieces per foot before hardware. The hinged forefoot remains separate. The single-main-body chassis alternative was not selected. Rail shape, connection geometry, and detailed fastener arrangements remain to be agreed.
- **2026-10-20 — Foot frame connections:** user accepted rectangular box-section rails with chamfered corners and inner mounting ledges; crossmembers lowered into locating recesses and secured by two recessed hex-socket bolts per end, eight total; a cradle seated across both rails with four top-access bolts; and separate washers with replaceable nut plates beneath the ledges. The twelve main bolts therefore have twelve washers. Nut plates install before the crossmember and cradle bolts, with access from above until the upper armor is fitted. Nut-plate count and retention, hardware sizes, and detailed joint geometry remain open.
- **2026-10-20 — Ankle construction:** user accepted a fixed cradle supporting an angular ring for forward/backward tilt, with a separate shin adapter inside the ring for sideways roll; four short pivot pins with replaceable bushings, thrust washers, and bolted retaining caps; a four-bolt keyed flange connecting the adapter to the shin beam; and one actuator per pivot. Accepted movement targets are 20 degrees forward/backward and 10 degrees toward either side, with stops and clearance checks still to be detailed. The adapter belongs to the foot-side mechanism; the shin specification now records the same interface.
- **2026-10-20 — Ankle hardware and actuator choice:** user accepted four sleeve bushings and four thrust washers, four retaining caps with two bolts each, and four separate actuator clevis brackets with four removable attachment pins and ball-jointed actuator eyes. Electric screw actuators with cylindrical bodies and extending rods are confirmed for the ankles. The user asked to reserve hydraulics for legs and knees; knee cylinders are recorded as hydraulic, with hydraulic hip cylinders as the working interpretation for other larger leg joints pending their detailed design.
- **2026-10-20 — Foot completion tracking:** user requested explicit notice when the foot and ankle details are finished. Added a checklist covering the remaining forefoot, sole and armor, dimensions and hardware inventory, service routing, assembly sequence, and clearance review. The foot and ankle remain in progress.
- **2026-10-20 — Forefoot construction:** user accepted a transverse hinge 2.00 m forward of the heel, leaving a 1.00 m moving forefoot, with the recorded hinge at Y = +1.20 m and Z = 0.35 m. The forefoot frame has two side rails, a front toe crossmember, and a rear hinge beam. One removable axle uses two sleeve bushings, two thrust washers, and a retaining cap with two bolts. An additional electric screw actuator drives flat-to-30-degree toe-up travel, bounded by mechanical stops. Total electric actuators per foot-and-ankle assembly are now three. Detailed stop faces, lever geometry, and clearance remain open.
- **2026-10-20 — Sole and armor attachments:** user accepted six replaceable composite pads in heel, midfoot, and forefoot pairs; separate rear and forefoot carrier plates; four recessed pad bolts with washers and compression sleeves per pad, totaling 24 of each; six rear-carrier bolts and four forefoot-carrier bolts; and five independently removable armor shells with four captive recessed bolts each, totaling 20 armor bolts. Detailed attachment geometry, thread sizes, and clearance review remain open.

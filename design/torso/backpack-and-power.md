## Confirmed design

### Rear torso, power, and cooling

- **Back profile:** keep the design simple, with a compact thruster backpack mounted to the rear torso structure. It remains a subassembly of the torso, preserving the six major body sections.
- **Power unit:** a cylindrical fictional power-core cartridge that plugs directly into a docking interface in the torso skeleton. The core stays with the torso when the backpack opens. Its energy-generation technology, internal mechanism, and output remain unspecified.
- **Power access:** the complete backpack lifts upward on a hinge along its top edge, revealing the core and serving as the rear access cover.
- **Core orientation and removal:** the cylindrical cartridge points front-to-back and slides straight out toward the rear from a keyed socket mounted in the torso frame.
- **Cooling appearance:** two tall recessed vertical vent strips flank the backpack on the fixed torso and remain visible when it is closed. They run parallel to the spine down to the pelvis as aligned upper and lower sections, with a clearance break at the articulated waist. Individual fin/slat geometry and internal cooling components remain open.
- **Rear thrusters:** two modest circular bell nozzles arranged side by side on the lower backpack, angled slightly rearward and downward. Keep the backpack and nozzle mouths clear of the waist's movement space. As with the calf thrusters, visual plausibility is the aim; no flight-performance requirement is implied.

### Backpack hardware and core retention

- **Hinges and supports:** two separated hinge brackets sharing the top horizontal pivot axis, with removable hinge pins and one short support strut on each side. The struts tuck inside when the backpack closes; exact geometry and opening travel remain open.
- **Closed-position retention:** two recessed mechanical latches at the lower corners, with locating blocks that seat the closed backpack against the torso frame. Latch internals and mounting hardware remain open.
- **Core retention:** keyed guide rails support and orient the cartridge during insertion from the rear and rearward removal. A rear retaining flange secured by recessed captive bolts holds it in the torso dock. Fastener count, guide geometry, and detailed docking connections remain open.
- **Backpack service routing:** protected flexible cable and service-line loops near the upper hinge, kept clear of the core extraction path. Service types, connector locations, and clearance through the opening motion remain open.

## Open design work

### Rear torso, power, and cooling refinement

- Dimensions and mounting geometry of the paired top hinges and side support struts; opening travel; lower latch mechanisms and locating blocks.
- Backpack opening clearance around the neck, shoulders, waist, and thruster nozzles.
- Cartridge dimensions, keyed docking socket, guide rails, retaining-flange geometry, captive-bolt count, and clearance along the rear-access installation/removal path.
- Packaging the core dock around the rear structural spine and central cockpit.
- Power and other service connections between the core, torso, and hinged backpack, including detailed routing of the protected loops near the upper hinge.
- Exterior vent dimensions and fin/slat geometry, internal cooling components, and routing.
- Detailed upper/lower vent-section mounts and clearance near the articulated waist and pelvis.
- Thruster mounts, service access, and nozzle geometry.
- Component inventory, part identifiers, fasteners, and assembly sequence.
